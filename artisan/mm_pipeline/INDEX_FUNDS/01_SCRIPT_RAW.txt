N22 | NARRATOR | YES | YES | 28 | Mistake one. Paying too much in fees. Some banks and insurance agents sell index funds with expense ratios above 1%. That is eighteen thousand two hundred dollars you paid for nothing. Check the expense ratio before buying. For an S&P 500 fund, anything above 0.20% is a ripoff.

N23 | NARRATOR | YES | YES | 25 | Mistake two. Panic-selling during a crash. When COVID hit in March 2020, the S&P 500 dropped 34%. Investors who sold everything missed the 68% recovery in the following 12 months. The crash felt terrifying. But index fund investors who did nothing got all their money back plus 34% extra within a year. Doing nothing was the winning move.

N24 | NARRATOR | YES | YES | 22 | Mistake three. Buying too many overlapping funds. Some investors buy ten funds that all track the same large US stocks. This does not improve returns. It creates messy taxes and tempts you to sell the funds that are down. Three funds cover everything: total US, total international, and bonds if you are near retirement.

N25 | NARRATOR | YES | YES | 25 | Mistake four. Waiting for a better time to start. A Schwab study found that investing all your money immediately beat waiting and dribbling it in 75% of the time between 2000 and 2024. The best days to invest are the ones that feel scariest — March 2009, April 2020. Waiting costs more than buying at the peak and holding.
