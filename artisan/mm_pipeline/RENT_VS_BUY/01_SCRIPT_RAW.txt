N10 | NARRATOR | YES | YES | 28 | Factor one: Time horizon. If Ryan sells at year three, closing costs going in and selling costs coming out wipe out his equity. Buying needs seven to ten years minimum to absorb transaction costs. The longer you stay, the more the fixed mortgage works in your favor.

N13 | NARRATOR | YES | YES | 22 | Factor four: Mobility and concentration. Steve can move tomorrow. Ryan has a house to sell or rent out. Steve owns thousands of companies. Ryan owns one house on one street. If the local economy tanks, Steve rebalances. Ryan cannot sell the upstairs bathroom.

H-E | HEADER | NO | NO | 6 | Part E — The Mistakes That Cost Six Figures

N14 | NARRATOR | YES | YES | 28 | Mistake one: Buying with a short time horizon. Three years of ownership can leave you underwater after transaction costs. The five percent rule: if you might move within five years, rent. The math only works if you stay long enough for appreciation to overcome the entry and exit fees.

N15 | NARRATOR | YES | YES | 25 | Mistake two: Renting but not investing. The entire renter advantage vanishes if you spend the monthly savings. A nicer car. A vacation. Lifestyle inflation eats the spread. The spreadsheet assumes discipline. Humans have other plans.

N16 | NARRATOR | YES | YES | 22 | Mistake three: Ignoring total cost of ownership. Ryan's mortgage is eighteen sixty-three. His real monthly cost is twenty-six fifty-three. Taxes. Insurance. Maintenance. The roof that needs twelve thousand dollars. The water heater that dies on a Saturday. Buying is not just a mortgage payment.

N17 | NARRATOR | YES | YES | 25 | Mistake four: Treating your home as an investment. It is an illiquid, concentrated asset with ongoing costs. You cannot sell the kitchen to fund retirement. Home equity is wealth, but it is not spendable wealth. Diversification matters.

H-F | HEADER | NO | NO | 6 | Part F — Your Decision Framework

N18 | NARRATOR | YES | YES | 25 | Ask yourself two questions. How long will you actually stay? Be honest. If the answer is less than seven years, rent. The math punishes short-term buyers. Second: will you genuinely invest the difference? If you rent, automate the investment. If you won't, buy. The forced savings of a mortgage beats the hypothetical returns of an undisciplined renter.

N19 | NARRATOR | YES | YES | 22 | The winner isn't the homeowner. The winner isn't the renter. The winner is the person who understands the tradeoffs and acts consistently. Ryan needed to stay. Steve needed to invest. Twenty years later, the difference wasn't rent versus buy. It was discipline versus drift.
